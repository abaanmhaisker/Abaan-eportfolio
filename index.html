My Engineering & Hackathon Journey

SIH 2025 → Sparsha NGO Project → SIH 2026

How our team grew from our first hackathon setback to building a real-world NGO platform and achieving internal selection in SIH 2026.

SIH 2025 Participant (Not Selected)

Rural Telemedicine Healthcare Platform

SIH 2025 was our team's first serious hackathon. We chose the rural telemedicine healthcare problem statement, worked hard under tight deadlines, spent long hours, and had sleepless nights trying to finish and improve our prototype. We faced the pressure of doing everything for the first time, gave our best effort, but were not selected in the internal selection round.

"SIH 2025 was difficult for us. It was disappointing, but it showed us where we needed to improve as a team. Not every fight is about winning. Sometimes you fight to learn, improve, and come back better."

Applied Learnings to a Real NGO Project
Sem 4 Vacation • May - June 2026 Non-Profit Social Project

Sparsha — NGO Student & Admin Portal

During our Semester 4 holidays, we did not want to sit idle or build something just for college marks. We found an opportunity to work with SPARSHA, an NGO social-service organization, to automate their daily administrative work without any profit motive. We built an end-to-end management portal tailored for their real operations:

  • Students: Quick access to personal profiles, attendance history, and exam performance.
  • Teachers / Volunteers: Logging daily sessions, marking attendance, and submitting field survey forms.
  • Admins: Centralized dashboard for managing users, custom forms, and tracking organizational reports.

"We did not want to build it just for profit. We wanted to create something useful for them, make their work easier, and learn from building a real project for real users."

Returned Stronger for SIH 2026
SIH 2026 Selected in Internal Round

CyberShield — Predictive Cash Egress Interception Platform (PS 184)

We came back in 2026 with the experience gained from SIH 2025 and our real-world work on Sparsha. We understood how to structure code better, divide responsibilities, and handle high pressure. Mentored by Prakash Parmar on Problem Statement 184, we worked hard through the long hours and tight deadlines again. Our preparation paid off and our team was selected in the internal round!

"We had already experienced the pressure once and built software for real users, so this time we came prepared. After going through the late nights and tight deadlines again, we felt genuinely happy when our team was selected in the internal round."
